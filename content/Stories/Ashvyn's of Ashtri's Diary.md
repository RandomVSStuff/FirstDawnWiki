_The 20th of December, of the First Year_

I have returned to my homelands after months of wandering, my feet weary but my heart fuller than when I left. The northernmost reaches of our lands still linger in my thoughts, their wild beauty haunting me as a half-remembered dream.

I departed in early November, when the snow had just begun to settle around the Great River. I thought I understood winter then, with its biting chill and frosted mornings. But the north taught me otherwise. There, the cold seeps not only into the flesh but into the soul itself, unyielding and unrelenting.

It was a realm unlike any I have known. Lights danced perpetually in the heavens, ribbons of green and purple twisting across the blackened sky. The sun, a shy stranger, lingered only briefly at the edge of the horizon, casting a pale glow before retreating once more. I would marvel at the silence, broken only by the crunch of snow beneath my boots or the distant howl of wind through barren trees.

This land is untamed, untouched. No man has laid claim to its icy expanse, and perhaps that is as it should be. Only the bears, white as the snow they tread upon, roam freely, their great forms blending seamlessly with the landscape. The lakes lie frozen, solid as stone, their depths locked away beneath layers of ice. I imagine it must remain this way year-round, unyielding in its harsh beauty.

I wished to linger, to learn from this land and its stillness, but my body betrayed me. A sickness crept upon me, slow at first, a cough that I brushed aside as weariness from the cold. But it grew worse. The chill seemed to take root within me, and I found myself coughing up blood, my strength waning. It was then I knew I must return.

Now, home feels both familiar and strange. I yearn for its warmth, yet I find myself hesitant to be seen. This sickness weighs upon me, not only in body but in spirit. I do not wish for others to worry, nor do I wish to bring any harm to them. And so, I will remain a traveler, even within the bounds of these lands I know so well.

Perhaps there is solace to be found in the forests and valleys of home, a gentler adventure to be had as I seek to regain my health. Until then, I will tread lightly, a shadow passing through the places of my youth, unseen and untroubled.

For now, I rest beneath the shelter of these words, the ink warming the page as my breath warms my hands.
